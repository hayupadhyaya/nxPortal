# PortalKit

The Swift package both apps share. Package root: `PortalKit/`.

#### test-network

- `LinkShaper(profile:seed:to:)` (`PortalTestSupport`): a UDP relay on loopback; `start()` returns the port a client sends to. It forwards to the Host's media port and returns the Host's datagrams to the client's address (the latest source it saw). Whole datagrams only; sealed bytes are never read.
- Host to client follows the whole `LinkProfile`; client to Host only loss and delay. `counts` (forwarded, dropped, reordered) is Host to client. `setProfile` applies from the next datagram; `setHostPort` for a port learned later (the `welcome`).
- Randomness: one SplitMix64 per direction, from `seed`; the same seed and input give the same drops (delay and jitter 0, since real time does not repeat). Order is kept unless `reorder` skips it.
- Profiles (`LinkProfile.named`, `names`):

| Name | What it does |
|---|---|
| `clean` | nothing |
| `lossy` | 1 % independent loss, 2 ± 1 ms |
| `bursty` | 0.3 % of datagrams start a run of 3...12 lost, 2 ± 2 ms |
| `stalls` | for the last 80 ms of every 1000 ms nothing passes, then all at once; 2 ± 1 ms |
| `narrow` | 25 Mbit/s bottleneck, 30 ms tail-drop queue (93 750 bytes), 2 ± 1 ms |

- With the rate controller (`client.md § rate`, the session's default, so every scenario but the fixed-rate ones below) `narrow` at a 20 Mbit/s ceiling starts at 10 and is gated: the last 2 s of `testBaselineForEveryProfile` put out 95 % of the frames sent. What follows is the fixed rate (`adaptiveRate: false`).
- `narrow` at 20 Mbit/s is 91 % of the link (21.3 Mbit/s of datagrams, 1.6 more of sound): the 4 x key frame cannot be placed however it is sent (unpaced `out=0/240`, 350 `nack`s, 85 frames given up, a key request every 100 ms; paced it still loses: the resends arrive into a full queue after the client's 26 ms give-up). That is a capacity question for the rate controller (a session starts at half its ceiling); the line is printed as `// baseline: the rate task raises this`. The gate is a 14 Mbit/s stream (65 % with its sound): the 117 KB key frame in one burst overflows the 94 KB queue, paced at 3 x the video rate it fits (`host.md § sender`): `out=243/240 nacks=0` in 8 of 8 runs; at 16 Mbit/s (74 %) about 1 run in 3 is short (the key frame plus the frames queued behind it leave the queue no room), so that is printed, not gated.
- `ScenarioTests` print `scenario <name>: out=… gap=… key=… nacks=… p95=…` for every profile at 20 Mbit/s, `narrow` at 14 Mbit/s with `paceMs` 4 (the default: gated, `framesOut` >= 99 %, `keyFrames` <= 2), 8 and 0, and at 16 with 4 and 0, and `clean` at `paceMs` 0 against 4 (4 s each: p95 11.7 against 15.7 ms; asserted: the rise is less than `paceMs` + 4 ms and the paced p95 under 24 ms).

- `random` (`LinkProfile.random(seed:)`, name `random`): for what nobody scripted. A generator seeded from the seed (each direction's lane has its own, also derived from it) draws the background once (loss 0...0.5 %, jitter 1...4 ms, which is also its base delay) and then, for ever, after a pause of 0.5...3 s that starts with the first Host to client datagram (not with the shaper: a Host that waited minutes for its client starts in a pause; after an idle gap of 3 s past a pause's end the next one is a pause from the new traffic, nothing is replayed), one of six: a loss run (the next 5...60 datagrams, either direction, ends with the last of them), a stall (20...400 ms, either direction, everything released at its end), a jitter spike (each datagram held 5...`extraMs` ms more, `extraMs` 5...40, for 0.2...2 s), a rate dip (Host to client: a bottleneck at 20...80 % of the Host to client rate of the last second, or since the first datagram when younger, never under 2 Mbit/s, 30 ms queue, 0.5...3 s; not drawn before half a second of traffic is known: "nothing" instead), a reorder spell (10 % of datagrams held 2...8 ms and overtaken, 0.5...2 s, either direction), or nothing (the next pause follows). Events begin and end on the clock of the datagrams that pass, in the order the seed drew them: the same seed and the same traffic (and clock) make the same run.
  - The seed is fresh for every tool run unless given (`--seed`, `LinkProfile.seed(for:given:)`) and is always printed: `shaper: profile=random seed=<n>`. `LinkShaper.seed` is the one in use (a profile made with `random(seed:)` carries its own); `events` = what began (`EventKind`, start, end), `profileInUse` = the drawn background; `LinkShaper(…, clock:)` takes a clock of the test's own (`RandomProfileTests` feed two shapers one synthetic sequence and compare the drops).
  - `LinkShaper(…, listenOnAllInterfaces: true)`: the client-facing socket is dual-stack on every interface instead of loopback IPv4 (what `portal-hostd --shape` uses, `host.md § hostd`).
- Soak: `ScenarioTests.testRandomNetworkSoak` runs 6 fresh seeds of `random`, 6 s each at 20 Mbit/s, one after another (about 40 s), and prints one line per seed `soak seed=<n>: out=<o>/<s> gap=<ms> key=<k> givenUp=<g> p95=<ms> events=<name@seconds from the window's start,…>` and `soak: worst gap=<ms> median gap=<ms> worst out=<pct>`. The numbers are for reading, not a gate; it asserts only that the session is alive at the end, frames came out in order, and a frame came out within 1 s after the last event ended. A seed that looks bad is replayed with `StreamScenario.run(profile: .random(seed: n), seconds: 6)`. `ScenarioResult` carries `seed`, `events`, `frameTimes`, `windowStartNs`, `windowEndNs` for this.
- Bottleneck: a FIFO that drains at `rateMbps`; a datagram that would take it past `rateMbps x queueMs` of bytes is dropped. A stall holds what arrives (up to the queue when a rate is set, else unbounded).
- `StreamScenario.run(..., bitrateMbps:, adaptiveRate:, profileChanges:, load:)`: `load` is the fake game's `FakeGame.Load` (below); `bitrateMbps` is the session's ceiling with `adaptiveRate` (default true) and the fixed rate without (the pacing tests and `narrow` at 14 / 16 run fixed); `profileChanges: [(atSeconds, LinkProfile)]` sets the shaper's profile at those times from the window's start (a scripted dip). `ScenarioResult.rateTrace` (the session's rate at the start and at every change, polled every 10 ms, on the clock of `frameTimes`), `cuts`, `rate(at:)`, `rateLine` (`rate start=… low=… end=… cuts=… trace=<s>:<Mbit/s>,…`), `sentTrace` (the game's frame count every 10 ms), `counts(fromSeconds:toSeconds:)` (frames sent and out in a part of the window), `cuts(fromSeconds:toSeconds:)`.
- Rate scenarios (`ScenarioTests`, `// MARK: the rate follows the link`): `narrow` with a 100 Mbit/s ceiling, 20 s (settles between 10 and 25, last 10 s 97 % out, no gap of 120 ms, one cut at most); `clean` with 100 (at least 90 within 12 s, no cut); a dip (40 ceiling, clean, 8 Mbit/s with a 30 ms queue from 8 s to 12 s, 20 s: no gap of 400 ms, the dip's last 2 s 95 % out, 3 key frames at most, back to 85 % of the level before by the end); `lossy`, `bursty`, `stalls` with 40, 10 s each (one cut at most; bursty's 100 ms gap gate, the 20 Mbit/s baseline's). A frame given up is repaired with a repair frame, not a 4 x key frame (`client.md § repair`): gates not met in every run (the dip's gap, bursty at 40's gap and share, lossy at 40's gap) keep their target as a non-strict expected failure beside a hard regression bound (the worst of 10 runs + 30 %; a share: the worst minus 1 point) (the design § 5, `todo.md`).
- Repair scenarios (`ScenarioTests`, `// MARK: a lost frame is repaired without a key frame`; `RepairTests`): `bursty` fixed at 50 Mbit/s, 20 s (one key frame in all, 98 % out, no escalation); `lossy` and `stalls`, 20 s each (no escalation); a frame lost for good and then its repair frame lost too (the picture restarts within 150 ms of that loss, no decode error). Every `ScenarioTests` run decodes with the real `VideoDecoder` (`ScenarioDecoding.real`).
- `StreamScenario.run(decoder:profile:seconds:…:dropVideo:repairDelay:longTermReferences:configure:)`: `decoder` makes the player's decoder for the run; each frame out of the session goes through it and the session hears `frameDecoded` (acknowledged to the Host) or `decodeFailed`, as from the app (without one nothing is acknowledged and the game repairs with key frames); `consume` replaces `decoder` with a loop that reports to the session itself (the fake client's `--decode`); `dropVideo` drops chosen fragments on arrival as if lost (`ClientSession.dropVideo`: the shaper cannot read a sealed fragment's frame or mark); `repairDelay` / `longTermReferences` are the fake game's. `ScenarioResult` adds `repairFrames`, `decodeErrors`, `ltrAcks` (acknowledgements the game got), `medianBytes` (ordinary, repair, key frames the game sent).
- `StreamScenario.run(profile:seconds:bitrateMbps:adaptiveRate:profileChanges:load:fps:seed:paceMs:silenceTimeout:midway:sessionMidway:configure:)`: fake game, `HostService`, `LinkShaper`, `ClientSession` in one process, each on its own ports and tap socket (`HostConfig.tapSocketPath`); returns `ScenarioResult` (`framesSent`, `framesOut`, `inOrder`, `longestGapMs` after the first second, `keyFrames`, `stats`, shaper counts, `alive`, `hostLog` = what the Host told the game). `paceMs` = the Host's pacing (default `HostConfig.defaultPaceMs`, 4; 0 = each frame at once, to compare). `line(name)` prints `scenario <name>: out=<out>/<sent> gap=<ms> key=<n> repair=<n> esc=<n> decodeErr=<n>`. The clock starts at the first frame (or after 5 s without one). A frame that comes out after the window's end is not counted (`framesOut`, gap); the gap is `StreamScenario.longestGapMs(times:windowStart:windowEnd:)`, a pure function that ignores times outside the window or out of order.
- `FakeGame(paddedToBitrate: true)` (`FakeLauncher(paddedToBitrate:)`): each frame gets one filler-data NAL unit (type 38) so it is `bitrate / 8 / fps` bytes, a key frame 4 times that, from the config as it stands (a later `config` of the same size changes the rate from the next frame, no new encoder and no key frame, as the tap); the decoder ignores it (`VideoDecoderTests`). Default off. `load:` (`FakeGame.Load`, from the first frame): `.bitrate` (default), `.quietUntil(seconds:)` (frames of 0.1 Mbit/s, whatever the config, until then: a still menu), `.halfEveryOtherSecond` (half the bitrate in even seconds: frames double in size every other second).
- The bottleneck queue holds every downstream datagram, pongs included (`testANarrowQueueDelaysThePongsToo`: a fixed 50 Mbit/s into `narrow` gives a round trip over 15 ms): the rate's ping floor sees the queue. A pause is not a queue: `testPausesAreNotCut` (`stalls`, and a 600 ms `hold` on `clean`, at a 40 Mbit/s ceiling) asserts no cut (the outage released at 200 Mbit/s).
- `LinkShaper.hold(forMs:towardClientOnly:releaseMbps:)`: holds everything (or the Host to client direction) for a time and releases it all at once, or at `releaseMbps` in arrival order (a 600 ms backlog at 40 Mbit/s, 3 MB released at once, overflows the loopback receive path: real loss); `StreamScenario.run(..., sessionMidway:)` runs once with the session at the middle (a key request during the hold); `ScenarioResult.frameCaptures` / `audioBlocks` carry capture times for the stale check.
- `StreamScenario.run(..., silenceTimeout:, hostSilenceTimeout:)`: `silenceTimeout` is the client's silence limit and the Host's too (`HostConfig.silenceTimeout`) unless `hostSilenceTimeout` is given; equal limits leave the order to the Host's 100 ms tick, so a test that asserts who ends first gives one side the shorter limit; `ScenarioResult` also carries `stallChanges` (the session's `stalls`, with times), `clientEnd` / `clientEndNs` (how and when it ended by itself), `hostPausedNs` (when the Host let the client go) and `nacksAtMidway`, all on the clock of `frameTimes`. `ScenarioTests` cut both directions with `setProfile(LinkProfile(loss: 1))` and restore the link from a task started in `midway`: a 4 s cut in 10 s (survives; `stalled` on at about 1 s and off at the end; first frame 39 ms after; 2 key frames; 0 nacks) and a cut past a limit, once per order (2 s against 3 s, the injected limit keeps each test at 8 s): the client's shorter ends `silence` 1.5...2.5 s after the cut and the Host lets go within 2.6 s; the Host's shorter ends the Host 1.5...2.6 s after the cut and the client `hostClosed`. The 10 s defaults are asserted where declared (`SessionTests`, `LoopbackTests`, `ClientModelTests`).
- `ScenarioResult.stallMs`: the largest overshoot of a 1 ms ticker in the test process from the session's `open` to the window's end (the process's own scheduling stall over the span its counts cover, `nacksSent` among them: launch, the first key frame and the window; the pairing and connect before `open` cannot raise a nack). `stallAtNs` says when it ended, `stallEvents` keeps every overshoot of 2 ms or more, `worstStall(excluding:)` leaves out a stretch where nothing can arrive (the 4 s cut: a link dead both ways shows no hole). `ScenarioTests.unstarved` judges the clean-link tests that assert no false alarm or a p95 bound (`testPacingCostsLessThanItsWindowOnACleanLink`, the `clean` run of `testBaselineForEveryProfile`, `testASessionSurvivesAFourSecondStall`: its `nacksSent == 0` is no false alarm on the clean parts around the cut, whose `stallMs` leaves out the cut) only on a run with every `stallMs` under 8 ms (above the assembler's 1 ms settle and 4 ms quiet tail, never raised); a starved run is repeated (the printed line says where in the window the worst stall ended), 5 attempts at most, and when all were starved the test FAILS ("machine too loaded to judge", stalls printed). Idle machine: the whole-run span (before the exclusion) read 9 to 10.3 ms in 4 of 13 attempts of the 4 s test; figures measured over the earlier, window-only span (1.1 to 7.6 ms over 40 runs) no longer apply.
- The scenario's session sends one neutral pad state after `launch`, which it re-sends every 8 ms as the app does: the client to Host lane carries a real client's traffic (a loss run counted in datagrams would otherwise silence nacks and key requests for most of a second).
- `ClientSession(mediaVia:)` sends media to that endpoint instead of the Host's; `mediaPortKnown` is told `welcome.udpPort` first.
- `portal-fakeclient --shape <profile>`: the shaper runs in the client process; an unknown name lists the valid ones, exit 64. `ScenarioTests` print one line per profile (the baseline later transport work is compared with).

#### versions

- `ProtocolVersion.tap` and `.network`, tap 2, network 4 -> `conventions.md § protocol-versioning`.

#### wire-tap

- Little-endian, packed in field order, no padding. `Bool` = 1 byte (0/1). `Float` = IEEE-754 bits as u32. `SIMD3` = x, y, z.
- Frame: `u32 length` (type + payload, 1...8 MiB), `u8 type`, payload. Trailing `Data` = rest of message.
- Error: bad length, short or extra bytes, a field out of range -> `WireError`; framer is dead after the first throw.
  A message of an unknown type is skipped by its length and handed to `push(_:skipped:)` (the Host counts it).

| Type | Name | Fields (bytes) |
|------|------|----------------|
| 0x01 | hello | version 2, titleID 16 (ASCII, 0x00 padded), nameLen 2, name UTF-8 |
| 0x02 | video | frame 4, captureNs 8, width 2, height 2, flags 1 (`FrameMark`: 0, 1 key, 2 repair; other values malformed), token 4 (i32, -1 none; opaque), data rest |
| 0x03 | audio | captureNs 8, channels 1, pcm rest |
| 0x04 | prepare | phase 1, done 4, total 4 (game to Host, before the first frame; `tap.md § prepare`) |
| 0x10 | config | maxWidth 2, maxHeight 2, maxFps 1, bitrate 4 |
| 0x11 | keyframe | none |
| 0x12 | pad | player 1, buttons 8, lx 4, ly 4, rx 4, ry 4 (i32) |
| 0x13 | motion | player 1, frame 1 (0 controller, 1 switchDevice), acc 12, rot 12 |
| 0x14 | players | mask 4 |
| 0x15 | pause | on 1 |
| 0x16 | quit | none |
| 0x17 | motionClear | player 1 |
| 0x18 | ltrAck | count 1 (1...16), token 4 (i32) x count (Host to game; `host.md § ltr`) |
| 0x19 | refresh | none (Host to game: a repair frame, or a key frame where the encoder cannot) |

#### wire-network

- Same field rules as `§ wire-tap`. Datagram bodies follow the 16-byte clear header; max datagram 1200, seal overhead 16.
- Datagram: `u8 type | u8 recoveryGroup (0) | u16 0 | u32 sessionID | u64 counter | ciphertext | 16-byte tag`; header is AEAD additional data.
- Video payload max 1147 (1200 - 16 - 16 - 21).

| Type | Name | Body (bytes) |
|------|------|--------------|
| 0x20 | video | frame 4, index 2, count 2, flags 1 (bit 0 key, bit 1 repair; 3 and other bits malformed: the tap's mark), captureNs 8, width 2, height 2, payload rest (count > 0, index < count) |
| 0x21 | audio | seq 4, captureNs 8, pcm rest |
| 0x22 | input | seq 4, player 1, buttons 8, lx 4, ly 4, rx 4, ry 4, hasMotion 1, if 1: frame 1, acc 12, rot 12 |
| 0x23 | keyRequest | lastGoodFrame 4 |
| 0x24 | ping | clientNs 8 |
| 0x25 | pong | clientNs 8, hostNs 8 |
| 0x26 | nack | frame 4, count 1 (0...64; 0 = every fragment of the frame), index 2 x count (client to Host; decoding refuses count > 64) |
| 0x27 | rate | bitsPerSecond 4 (client to Host: the video rate wanted now, below the hello's wish; `client.md § rate`, `host.md § service`) |
| 0x28 | ack | frame 4 (client to Host: the newest frame the player decoded; at most 30 a second, always for a key or repair frame; `client.md § repair`) |
| 0x2A | repair | lastGoodFrame 4 (client to Host: a frame was given up, a restart point is wanted; the Host passes the tap `refresh`) |
| 0x29 | hostStats | framesFromGame 4, framesSent 4, framesDropped 4, resent 4, rateBps 4, sendLateMaxUs 4 (Host to client, once a second; counts since the session began; `HostStats`) |
| 0x2B | load | flags 1 (bit 0 GPU present, bit 1 game alive, other bits malformed), thermal 1 (0...3), pressure 1 (0...2), cpuTotal 2, cpuPerf 2, cpuEff 2, gpu 2 (tenths of a percent, 0xFFFF n/a), memUsedMiB 4, memTotalMiB 4, gameCpuCentiCores 4, gameMemMiB 4, gameFpsDeci 2, frameMeanDeciMs 2, frameP95DeciMs 2, frameMaxDeciMs 2 = 35 bytes (Host to device, about once a second to a playing session at the panel's "full"; `HostLoad`; sent on the sender's lazy slot, never retransmitted; `host.md § load`; pinned by `testLoadBytesAreThePinnedVector`) |

- `PadButtons` bits 0...15: A B X Y LS RS L R ZL ZR Plus Minus Left Up Right Down.
- `hello` JSON: `version`, `clientKey`, `ephemeral`, `kind`, `maxWidth`, `maxHeight`, `maxFps`, and `bitrate` (optional, UInt32 bit/s: the wished video bitrate; the key is absent when the client has no wish). Network version 3 (`nack`, `rate`, `ack`, `hostStats`, `repair`, the fragment's flags byte).
- Network version 4 (not shipped; the app-polish plan adds to it): `GameEntry.art: ArtRef?` (`hash` 64 hex SHA-256 of the PNG, `bytes`); `artRequest(titleID:hash:)` -> `art(titleID:hash:index:count:bytes:)` chunks `0..<count` in order, `ArtChunking.chunkBytes` 48 KiB raw (under 70,000 bytes sealed and framed), `ArtChunking.maxBytes` 512 KiB, `count` = `ArtChunking.count(bytes:)`, `count` 0 = no such art. A device's `artRequest` with a `titleID` over 64 bytes of UTF-8 or a `hash` not exactly 64 bytes of UTF-8 (a hash is 64 ASCII hex characters) is dropped with no reply and no queue entry (`HostService.maxArtTitleBytes`). Host: `host.md § library`; device: `client.md § model`.
- Network version 4 (also): `liveSettings(LiveSettings)` (device to Host; `LiveSettings { ceilingBps: UInt32?, panel: PanelLevel? }`, `PanelLevel` = `off` / `fps` / `compact` / `full`, both keys optional and absent when nil, pinned as `liveSettings` and `liveSettings.empty` in `Vectors.swift`). Admitted phase only: before `ready` it ends the connection like any other unexpected message. Host rules: `host.md § service`; the device sends it after every `ready` and on each change (`client.md § model`).
- Network version 4 (also): `GameState.render: RenderSize?` (`RenderSize { width, height: UInt16, exact: Bool }`): the newest picture's size under the device's cap (`exact` = under the cap on both sides, `RenderSize.fitted`: the tap fits by aspect, so a side that reaches the cap, within 2 for its even rounding, makes it a lower bound); absent before the first picture and after the game ends. Pinned by `WireTests` and the `gameState.render` vector.
- Network version 4 (also): `GameState.preparing: Preparing?` (`Preparing { phase: UInt8, done: UInt32, total: UInt32 }`, the tap's numbers; `Preparing.waiting` = 0 / 0 / 0, `total` 0 meaning "preparing, nothing to count"); the key is absent when not preparing. Host rules (`PreparingTracker`) -> `host.md § service`. `Preparing.fraction` (0...1) and `Preparing.percent` (rounded, 100 only when `done >= total`, 0 for `total` 0) are the one rule for "preparing N %" on the device and in the Mac's menu (`PreparingTests`). Pinned by `WireTests` (round trip with an unknown phase, absent key decodes nil and is never written).
- Network version 4 (also): `bye(takenBy: String?)` (Host to device; pinned as `bye.takenBy` = `{"bye":{"takenBy":"TV"}}`, `bye` without the key unchanged): the Mac is now in use on that device (the shown name); the device does not reconnect by itself (`client.md § model`).
- Network version 4 (also): `Hello.resume: Bool?` (absent when nil, pinned as `hello.library` = `"resume":false` in `Vectors.swift`; `hello` and `hello.bitrate` carry no key). `false` = a library session, absent or `true` = resuming as before; rules `§ session-rules`, Host `host.md § service`, device `client.md § model`.
- Network version 4 (also): `Hello.name: String?` (pinned as `hello.name`; no key when nil): the device's cleaned name (`DeviceName.clean`: control and format characters removed except U+200C / U+200D, white space collapsed, trimmed, cut to 40 characters and 120 bytes at a character boundary; idempotent). `pairStart.name` carries the same cleaned name. The Host cleans it again and stores its own result; it never trusts the wire. Names it sends back (`busy(device:)`, `bye(takenBy:)`) are the shown name, cleaned and bounded at the moment of sending, and the receiving app cleans them again. Unsealed (`pairStart`, `hello`, `refuse`). Limits (CLIP, never refuse: the pairing or session goes on): `Hello.name` and `pairStart.name` are cleaned and cut to 40 characters / 120 bytes by the Host; a name with nothing visible (only joiners, fillers, a Braille blank, private-use or unassigned scalars) is no name (the old one stays; a new pairing is "Device"). The Host keeps a hello's name only after the device proved its key (`host.md § pairing-store`). Bounded on decode (`ControlMessage(decoding:)`): MALFORMED (the connection ends) a `hello` / `pairStart` key or ephemeral over `ControlMessage.keyBytes` (32); CLIP a `hello` / `pairStart` name to 40 characters / 120 bytes at a character boundary (`DeviceName.clip`, before any cleaning); REFUSE (ignored, the session goes on) a `launch` whose `titleID` is not a title ID (`TitleID.isWellFormed`), so a "not found" answer echoes at most 32 bytes. `DeviceModelName` (no UIKit) maps the hardware identifier to the model's short name.
- Network version 4 (also): `Hello.startBps: UInt32?` (bit/s; absent when nil, pinned as `hello.start` in `Vectors.swift`; the other hellos carry no key): the rate the device starts at, the value of its first `rate` (its rate controller's start; no key with a fixed rate). The Host clamps it as a `rate` and keeps it as the session's rate from admission, so the game's first `config` carries it whatever the order of `launch`, the game's tap `hello` and the first `rate` (`host.md § service`, `client.md § rate`).
- Network version 4 (also): per-game options, device to Host `optionsRequest(titleID:)`, `setOption(titleID:env:value:)` (`value` absent = remove the choice), `resetOptions(titleID:)`, Host to device `options(GameOptions)` (also the answer to the other two). `GameOptions { titleID, settings: [OptionSetting], rejected: String? }`; `OptionSetting { env, kind, section, label, note, defaultValue, current: String; chosen: Bool; options: [[String]]?; min, max, step: Double?; on, off: String?; base: OptionBase?; level, needs: String? }` (optional keys absent when nil); `kind` is the launcher's text (`choice`, `range`, `toggle`; another stays as it is); `OptionBase { docked, handheld: Size }`, `Size` = `[width, height]` as in `launcher.json`. Admitted phase only (before `ready` it ends the connection), library or playing session; bounded on decode on both sides (`OptionLimits`, one definition, `TitleID.isWellFormed` for titles): refused = `WireError.refused`, the message is ignored and the session goes on (Host after admission; a device); anything else undecodable still ends it. REFUSE (whole message): a title that is not a title ID, a name over 64 bytes or not `DM_` + `A-Z 0-9 _`, a value, `on`/`off` over 128 bytes, a kind or level over 32, a `needs` over 200, a text over 2 KB, a control character in any text, over 200 rows or 64 choices (a choice is exactly `[value, label]`), `min` > `max`, a step <= 0, a number that is not finite, a size outside 1...100 000, a `rejected` over 64 bytes. CLIP/DROP (the Host reader only, from `launcher.json`, never on the wire): texts lose control characters and are cut at 512 characters; a long kind drops the row; a long level, `needs` or value, or a size outside 1...100 000, is left out; a range with `min` over `max` or `step` <= 0 loses its numbers, so the reader never produces what the decoder refuses (`testWhatTheReaderProducesAlwaysRoundTripsThroughTheDecoder`). A device whose `options` reply is refused gets one event (`ClientSession.optionRefusals`), treats it as the answer to its oldest unanswered options message (the Host answers in order), and its calls also stop waiting after the session's reply time. `GameOptions.full` (optional key, true = the Mac keeps choices for as many games as it can and this one has none yet; pinned as `options.full`). Pinned as `optionsRequest`, `options`, `options.rejected`, `setOption`, `setOption.remove`, `resetOptions` in `Vectors.swift`; 25 settings stay under 40 KB, the reader limits keep any list far under the 1 MiB message limit. Rules: `host.md § options`.
- Control (TCP): `u32 length` (1...1 MiB) + JSON; `ControlMessage.encoded()` / `init(decoding:)` (sorted keys, `Data` base64), `ControlFramer`.
- Control JSON shape (pinned in `Tests/PortalKitTests/Vectors.swift`): a case is an object with one key, the case name; labelled payloads are keyed by label (`{"launch":{"titleID":"..."}}`), an unlabelled payload by `_0` (`{"pairProof":{"_0":"<base64>"}}`), a case without payload has `{}` (`{"ready":{}}`); an optional field is absent when nil. Enums with raw values are their string (`kind`: `phone` `tablet` `tv` `tool`; `run`: `idle` `starting` `running` `paused`). Renaming a case, label or field is a wire change.
- Send: `ControlFramer.framed(_:)` throws `WireError.tooLarge` over `ControlFramer.maxMessage` (1 MiB, the reader's limit); `frame(_:)` does not check (existing callers).
- Vectors: `VectorTests` pins every control message, every datagram and tap message of the stable set, one sealed datagram, the pairing proofs and the four session keys; the audio and video datagrams, the tap video message and the tap hello are pinned when the transport plan lands. A vector changes only with a version bump and a `CHANGELOG-protocol.md` entry.

#### pairing

- Code: 6 digits, uniform 0...999999, zero-padded; single use, 3 tries, 120 s (`PairingLimits`).
- Keys on the wire: 32 raw X25519 public-key bytes.
- Pairing key: `HKDF<SHA256>` over `ECDH(eph, eph)`, salt = challenge's 16 random bytes, info `"nxportal pair"`.
- Client proof: `HMAC<SHA256>`(pairing key) over code UTF-8 `||` clientKey `||` clientEph `||` hostKey `||` hostEph. Host proof: same input `||` `"host"`.
- Host `verify` order: locked or already paired -> `.locked`; past lifetime -> `.expired`; wrong/no challenge uses a try (0 left -> `.locked`); correct -> `.ok`. Constant-time compare.
- Known limit: someone already on the home network who actively intercepts the 2-minute pairing exchange could guess the code. A stronger exchange can replace it later without changing anything else.

#### sealing

- Session keys: `HKDF<SHA256>`, IKM = `ECDH(static, static) || ECDH(eph, eph)`, salt = clientEph `||` hostEph, info `"nxportal v1 "` + `c2h ctl` / `h2c ctl` / `c2h media` / `h2c media`, 32 bytes each.
- ChaCha20-Poly1305; nonce = 4 zero bytes + u64-LE counter from 0, one counter per direction.
- Stream: `ciphertext || tag`, counter implicit; opener takes only the next counter, advances on success.
- Datagram: header layout in `§ wire-network`; opener checks length, session, replay window (64 wide), then tag; window moves only after the tag verifies.
- A sender with several queues seals at send time, in the order datagrams leave (the Host's `MediaSender`): sealing at enqueue would let a datagram overtaken by 64 others be refused as replayed.

#### secrets

- `SecretStoring` (`read` / `write` / `delete` by key; `Sendable`): `KeychainSecrets(service:)` (default `nxportal`; generic password, this device only, after first unlock; data-protection keychain on iOS / tvOS only; no unit test, exercised in the apps), `FileSecrets(directory:)`, `MemorySecrets` (tests, previews). Errors: `SecretStoreError.keychain(status)` / `.file(operation, errno)`.
- `FileSecrets`: one file per key, name = key; key = `[a-z0-9.-]+`, not starting with `.`, no `..`, else `.file("bad key", EINVAL)`. Directory created 0700; a value is written to a hidden temporary file (0600) and renamed over the key, so a reader sees the old or the new value; a missing key reads nil, deleting one is not an error.
- Redaction: `Identity`, `SessionKeys`, `PairingHost`, `PairingClient` and the four sealers / openers print `Name(<redacted>)` by `description`, `debugDescription` and `dump` (empty mirror). Never add a stored property to them that a log line should show.
- `Identity.discoveryID(publicKey:)` (and `identity.discoveryID`): first 8 bytes of SHA-256 of a public key as 16 lower-case hex digits; the Host advertises it as TXT `id` (`host.md § admin`), a client computes it from a stored Host key.
- Known-answer tests reach the random parts of pairing through internal overloads: `PairingClient.start(ephemeral:)`, `PairingHost.challenge(for:ephemeral:salt:)`.

#### video

- Split: consecutive `index` from 0, same `count`/`key`/`repair`/`captureNs`/size; every payload but the last is 1147 bytes; empty frame = 1 empty fragment; more than 65535 fragments traps.
- Video data: 4-byte big-endian length-prefixed HEVC NAL units (VideoToolbox output). NAL type = `(firstByte >> 1) & 0x3F`; `parameterSets` = first VPS 32, SPS 33, PPS 34.
- `HEVC.nalUnits` -> nil on overrun or short length; empty input -> `[]`. `annexB` swaps each length for `00 00 00 01`; malformed -> empty.
- Assembler (`FrameAssembler`, a pure value type; time comes from the caller as `nowNs`, the round trip as `rttNs`): `push(_:nowNs:)` and `tick(nowNs:rttNs:)` return `[AssemblerEvent]` = `.frame`, `.nack(frame:indices:)`, `.refresh(lastGood:)`; `nextTickNs(rttNs:)` = when `tick` next has work (nil while nothing is missing); `restart()` for a new stream; `counts` (`AssemblerCounts`) = totals since it was made or restarted.
- Window: the newest 8 frame numbers (plain `UInt32`). Fragments in any order. Frames come out strictly in order: a non-key frame only right after the frame before it; a complete key frame at any time (incomplete frames before it are dropped).
- Missing: indices below the highest index received of the frame; every index not received once any fragment of a later frame has arrived (also for a frame entered below a later one), or once no fragment of the frame has arrived for 4 ms (`tailQuietNs`: the Host sends a frame back to back, so a quiet partial frame has lost its tail) and only when some Host datagram of any kind (sound, pong, `hostStats`) arrived at least 4 ms after the frame's last fragment (`tick(heardNs:)`; silence from everything is a stalled radio, so nothing is asked during one; a later frame's fragment still asks at once; the session ticks on every non-video datagram while a tail is open); such a tail is asked for at once, but its give-up clock starts only when a later frame arrives (a stall holds everything and must not give frames up); a frame number skipped over (no fragment at all) is missing whole.
- `nack` timing:
  - A hole inside a frame (a higher index arrived) is recorded with its time and asked for by the first `tick` at least 1 ms later (`settleNs`) if still open: a swap of two datagrams heals without a `nack`.
  - A hole shown by a later frame (the tail of a frame, a whole skipped frame) is asked for at once, from `push`.
  - Again after `max(2 x rtt, 8 ms)` while still missing, every known missing index; at most 3 such asks a frame (first + 2 retries). A hole found after the first ask is asked for once on its own (not counted as a round; it does not move the retry clock).
  - Indices split 64 per `nack`; a frame skipped over is `nack(frame, [])` (all of it).
- Give up: `min(settle + 2 x retry + rtt + 5 ms, 50 ms)` (settle 1 ms, retry `max(2 x rtt, 8 ms)`: every one of the three asks can be answered before the frame is given up, until the 50 ms cap; rtt 1 ms -> 23 ms, 4 ms -> 26 ms, 8 ms -> 46 ms) after the frame was first seen missing something -> frames up to and including it are dropped, `.refresh(lastGood:)` (last frame put out); only a restart point comes out next.
- Late: a fragment of a frame given up within 200 ms counts in `fragmentsLate` and marks it in `givenUp` (`GivenUpFrame`: frame, when, missing since, late; kept 200 ms and until settled, 64 at most). `settledGiveUps(nowNs:)` returns, once each, the frames given up 150 ms or more ago with no late fragment: what the rate controller counts (`client.md § rate`).
- Awaiting a restart point (fresh assembler, after a give-up, `awaitKeyframe()`): a key frame, or a repair frame while `acceptsRepair` (the caller sets it once a key frame decoded; a fresh assembler and `awaitKeyframe()` clear it). Frames before the first restart point in the window are dropped and not asked for; a frame with no restart point before it is not kept; each such frame counts once in `framesWaited` (never given up one by one, never decoded). A complete repair frame the output waits for comes out at once, the frames before it dropped (as for a key frame); `restartsAtRepair` counts it. The decoder is not flushed at a repair frame. `.refresh` is given once per wait (also for the first non-key fragment of a fresh assembler); `awaitKeyframe()` (the caller asked itself) gives none. Out of a wait a repair frame is an ordinary next frame.
- Ignored: duplicates, fragments of frames already put out, given up or dropped (a given-up one's still counts as late, above).
- A frame number more than the window ahead of the next frame needed gives up everything before the window (bounded work: at most 7 frames asked for whole).
- Count 0, index >= count, `count * 1147 > maxFrameBytes` (default 8 MiB, the tap's largest frame; nothing allocated), or fragments disagreeing on count/key/size: the frame is given up; its later fragments are ignored. Such a fragment more than the window ahead of the newest frame held (or the last put out) is ignored instead (giving up through it would freeze the stream).
- Work per fragment is constant (no scan of the frame, no copy of its slots); a `nack` costs the frame's missing indices.
- Client timer and stats: `client.md § session`, `§ stats`; Host resends: `host.md § service`.

#### session-rules

- `HostSessionModel` (`Session/`): pure value type; no timers or I/O; time only via `now`.
- No session identity: the caller reports `clientLeft` only for the live connection (`host.md § service`). Returned actions are assumed done (`pauseGame` sets `gamePaused`, `resumeGame` clears it).
- `playing`: the live session plays (admitted resuming with a game present that is not left paused (`admit(gameExists:)`; the Host passes `game != nil`), or it sent `launch` / `resume`); the Host relays media and lets an owed pause go only by it. A library session (`Hello.resume == false`) is told the truth and plays only once it asks.
- Defaults: `inputTimeout` 0.5 s, `silenceTimeout` 10 s (`HostSessionModel.defaultSilenceTimeout`; a stall of up to 9 s keeps the session), `awakeGrace` 600 s.
